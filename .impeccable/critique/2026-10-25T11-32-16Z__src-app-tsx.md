## Overall Impression

The opening tour is the strongest moment on the page. The biggest flaw is structural: the 3D is a fixed layer that changes pose at the midline, so for half of every transition the old mesh sits over the incoming panel (or cuts out), which breaks the owner's Enerblock rule about 14 times per scroll-through. The single biggest opportunity: make each panel carry its own view of the mesh.

## What's Working

1. The opening tour teaches the product in one gesture: name, standing panel, built mesh, camera between the faces with face/fibre labels; the name docking onto its black plate settles cleanly.
2. Innovation is explanation first, figure second, with a real comparison: axes, the automatic flat-mesh preview with its toggle, then the mineral close-up; slab headings read on both grounds and layouts vary as asked.
3. Why and the copy voice: a full-screen statement, then the timed strike list ending on the black RTARIUM bar. Persuasive without overclaiming. Measured contrast is strong (13.5:1 yellow on black, 8.4:1 secondary).

## Priority Issues

[P1] The 3D does not travel with its panel. The canvas is fixed and switches pose at mid-viewport, so during each transition the previous mesh covers the incoming panel ("Three elements." prints over the No-coating close-up) or cuts out leaving a blank half-screen; outgoing copy also fades (.scene__inner opacity) and the fog colour damps to the global ground. Why: the owner's direction says no cross-fades, no colour washes on the mesh, no text/3D overlap. Fix: clip the stage to the owning panel's visible rect (render the incoming pose into the incoming panel during a transition), drop the opacity term so copy only translates, take fog colour from the panel's own ground. Suggested command: /impeccable animate.

[P1] The name is illegible through the first scroll. Between about p 0.2 and 0.9 the difference-blended RTARIUM sits on the dense mesh as an olive grid; the black plate only opens from p 0.55, so "TARIUM" is unreadable until the end, and the olive negative is off-palette. Why: "mesh and type must both read", and this is every visitor's first interaction. Fix: open the plate with the shrink (≈ p 0.08–0.4), or build the mesh up from below so it reaches the name only after the plate is in. Suggested command: /impeccable animate.

[P1] Phones strip labels, hints and caveats. .race__more p span:last-child hides the title along with the reading ("79.6 GPa", "3.40 %" alone); .spec__hint and .readout__show are hidden so tap-to-see is undiscoverable; .cast__note is hidden so +77 % appears without "Figures describe the tested materials…". Why: breaks principles 3 and 5; specifiers open forwarded links on phones. Fix: hide only the reading sentence, keep a one-line hint under "The sheet.", keep the caveat as a micro caption. Suggested command: /impeccable adapt.

[P2] Tech Space's signature interaction is under-signalled, and Yarn/Concrete leave the brand language. Readouts look static; the night mesh reads near-black; the "15 mm" pin lands on "All figures"; Yarn and Concrete have no mesh and no slab headline; the x-ray mesh is yellow; numbers switch faces. Fix: a low-emphasis "Show on the mesh" line by default and the hint above the left column; warmer night lighting; one face for all measurements; a Yarn slab headline with a camera move into one roving; Concrete as a cut section in bronze. Suggested command: /impeccable clarify, then /impeccable typeset.

[P2] Chrome legibility: progress fill and Applications numerals. The active progress label is 1.22–1.27:1 for 2.4 s on every screen and pending chips are 1.2:1 against the ground; Applications numerals are 1.4–3.9:1 over photos; Tech Space chip borders 1.62:1. Why: PRODUCT.md commits to WCAG 2.2 AA; both the review and the detector caught these. Fix: tie the label colour to the fill position, plate or drop the numerals, raise chip borders to ≥3:1. Suggested command: /impeccable polish.
